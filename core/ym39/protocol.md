# Methods and scope

## What would count as evidence?

This is an exploratory falsification exercise. The logical alternative is a sequence of normalized physical excitations orthogonal to the vacuum whose excess energies tend to zero *in the target theory*. An arbitrarily small contribution to a particular measured correlator is a different hypothesis. A recovered artificial injection tests the measurement and analysis only. A mode with energy falling at successively smaller momentum would merit follow-up at fixed physical scale, larger volume and finer spacing before any continuum inference.

## Previously acquired correlators (YM38)

Use the 4³ extra and 8³,16³ confirmation samples, lags 1–5 normalized by lag zero, periodic temporal length 16. Use 64-sweep blocks and delete-one-block jackknife covariance of the normalized correlation vector. The finite-volume pilot is a separate no-refit prediction check, with a noisy covariance. The primary approximate uncertainty ellipsoid uses the χ² quantile for 5 components at 1−0.05/3. It is a data uncertainty region. Fitted-model residuals are not being presented as χ² p-values. Also report the Hotelling finite-sample F reference cutoff, exact for independent Gaussian vector means and only a sensitivity reference here because normalized correlators are ratios and blocks may remain correlated. The Gaussian and blocking approximations, particularly the small number of blocks at large volume, limit coverage.

Use the normalized positive kernel K(E,t)=[exp(−Et)+exp(−E(T−t))]/[1+exp(−ET)]. The test grid for nuisance energies has adjustable nonnegative weights on lattice dispersions E(p,m)=2 asinh(sqrt(sin²(p/2)+sinh²(m/2))), with masses 0.8 through 4 at step 0.1, and 5,7,10,20. Its lower threshold and grid are analysis choices. For a candidate with assigned initial fraction f, find the closest mixture to the data in covariance-whitened space. A realized mixture gives an upper distance and a supporting hyperplane gives a lower distance to the finite convex hull. Weights can vary by volume. Such a fit is a correlator construction, not a reconstruction of an SU(2) Hamiltonian.

Test four hypothetical signals. (1) One massless line with unit limiting speed. (2) One line at half the speed, a detector sensitivity control with no claim of Lorentz invariance. (3) A tower with rest masses 1/j and weights 2^(−j), accumulating at zero. Its first 48 terms are used and the residual weight ≤2^(−48) is assigned to term 48. (4) Positive weights on a countable dense set of rest masses in (0,1). At dyadic denominator 2^j all odd numerators share total weight 2^(−j). Truncate at j=16, assigning the remaining weight to the j=16 distribution, for an absolute error at most 1/65536 in each normalized time slice before multiplication by f. This differs from a 1/j tower, whose only accumulation point is zero. Fractions are percentages of the equal-time signal in this operator. For every alternative use f=0, .01, .05, .10, .25 and 1. For replication on the pilot, weights are fixed at their original fitted values.

An independent grid-free inequality uses positivity: for 0≤E≤E*, K(E,t)≥K(E*,t). Hence W(E≤E*)≤min_t C(t)/(C(0)K(E*,t)). Form nominal simultaneous upper limits by union bound over 3 sizes × 5 lags, with one-sided Student margins on blocked ratios. Also rerun with blocks 32 and 128 and only lags 1–3. No nuisance spectral grid is needed. Finite temporal extent can include thermal transitions. All bounds concern the operator-weighted spectral signal, and none bounds the number of states in the spectrum.

For a separate injection check resample blocks within each of the two chains, 400 replicates per size. Add f(K_light−observed_mean_ratio) to each resampled vector. This injects the specified mean shift while retaining the original absolute noise. Ask whether the gapped nuisance grid alone is now excluded. These are resampled, synthetic power diagnostics, not new physical observations; their results depend on nuisance flexibility and the baseline.

## Fresh Euclidean Wilson simulations

Use YM38's isotropic SU(2) Wilson heatbath update. Temporal period is now 24. Use L=8,16 and β=2.35,2.45, hot and cold starts, 256 warmup sweeps; collect 2048 sweeps per chain at L=8 and 1024 at L=16. For integer B=100β, seed=390000+100B+2L+i with i=0,1 for cold,hot. Spatial smearing uses three simultaneous steps with weight 0.5. Seven probes on the same configurations: scalar fundamental squares and 2×2 squares at p=2π/L and at rest; a difference of transverse orientations at p=2π/L and at rest; and a spatial winding trace at rest. Average over axes and time origins. Winding flux is a distinct center-charge sector. It can expose finite-box states and must not be read as a local glueball mass. SU(2) gauge invariance is checked after random local rotations on every new correlator. The ensemble column means from 16 consecutive sweeps are stored. For a scalar at rest subtract the square of its ensemble mean, recomputing the subtraction in each jackknife replicate.

Report an exploratory effective-energy plateau only when all of C(1..3) exceed three standard errors, cosh-ratio energies in (1,2) and (2,3) agree within two correlated errors, and hot/cold tests pass for C(1..3) and the plaquette (within three combined errors), with both 32- and 64-sweep blocks. Do not turn undetected signals into zero energy. The quoted late-window errors are one statistical standard error. This screening of many channels is exploratory, without a discovery-level multiplicity calibration. Longer-time, thermal and operator-basis systematics remain. The second β is a robustness check. The volumes have not been matched in physical units across β.

Additional sensitivity test on the two scalar probes at rest: compute simultaneous approximate positive-weight upper limits below aE=0.1, using one-sided Student margins over 50 lag/probe/ensemble comparisons (including the extension). Both probes use an ensemble vacuum subtraction. Use these limits only for those observables. Persisting finite-sample vacuum-subtraction error, thermal transitions, relaxation times and blocking error are not covered by these nominal margins.

## Independent choice of a two-operator detector

For the β=2.35 moving scalar probes replay the same chains and seeds to recover their symmetric cross-correlation. This replay adds the missing off-diagonal observable, not independent samples. Solve C(1)v=λC(0)v on the cold chain, choosing the largest generalized eigenvalue. Freeze v and measure its projected correlation on the hot chain, then reverse the roles. Quote conditional jackknife uncertainty on the held-out chain; the two folds are not independent estimates and are not averaged. An exact synthetic two-state positive control verifies that the method isolates a weak state when the operator overlaps differ enough. A second independent L=16 ensemble at β=2.35 (seeds 430016/430017, 320 warmup and 3072 measurements each) was added after the first L=16 batch at this coupling failed all standalone two-window screens. Report the extension separately, retaining failed original outcomes.

## Logical controls

If an admissible *correlator model* with a positive gap lies inside the error region, adding a positive component of arbitrarily small weight changes each normalized lag by at most that weight. With a strict interior fit there is always a small positive addition that remains compatible, even if its energies accumulate at zero. An explicit example at rest adds Σ 2^(−j)exp(−t/j) in infinite Euclidean time. For each j this correlation is bounded below by its jth term; its exponential rate tends to zero at long time. On finitely many noisy times the admixture can be made arbitrarily small. This argument concerns identifiability; it does not show that the altered spectrum is realized in pure SU(2). Conversely, gauge copies, the vacuum component of an arbitrary wavefunction, or massless photons of an added electromagnetic field cannot provide a physical pure-Yang–Mills counterexample.

## Reproduction

From core/ym39 build with g++ -O3 -std=c++17 probes.cpp -o probes. Run ./probes 4 125 0 0 check 2.35 both. Production arguments: L SEED WARMUP SAMPLES START BETA [cross|both]. Use cross on the β=2.35 replays and both for the extension. Run python3 controls.py for tests or python3 controls.py --json for all profiles, specific witness weights, cross-checks, injection curves and numerical uncertainties. Python 3, NumPy and SciPy are required. The GitHub check recomputes the results from the saved blocks, including the prior YM38 measurements. It does not rerun every Monte Carlo update.

The same positive-mixture stress test at zero momentum uses the connected rest-square and rest-large-square data at T=24, with heavy energies ≥0.8, versus a line at 0.1 or a 1/j tower (individual D² values are exploratory across channels). The additional finite-box symmetry check flips the sign of all links crossing a periodic sheet in each spatial direction. The action, contractible loops and their correlators are invariant. Each once-winding observable changes sign. In a center-symmetric vacuum all local contractible probes therefore have exactly zero matrix element to the corresponding odd-flux sector. This is an exact example of a blind operator inside finite-volume pure SU(2). It gives no upper bound tending to zero on the energy of that sector. In the separate Hamiltonian model with only the electric Casimir term and coefficient α>0, the winding trace on a loop of L distinct edges has electric excitation 3αL/4 by the link Casimir, whereas a square costs 3α. This boundary-condition sector need not survive as a finite-energy excitation of the continuum theory on infinite space.


The archive measurement_blocks.zip contains the full 14 headerless block CSVs for the new simulation. The reader can open the ZIP directly. The eight primary files have first column mean plaquette followed by seven groups of nine (operator mean, then correlations at time separations 0–7); four cross files contain only the eight symmetric cross-correlations. The two extension files contain both in that order. Each row covers sixteen consecutive measured sweeps. The archive contains block averages, not individual gauge configurations.

The elementary discrete_examples control compares levels k/N and 1+k/N above a common zero for k=1,…,N. Both grids get arbitrarily fine. Their first energies converge to 0 and 1 respectively. Changing the common energy origin by 17 leaves both gap sequences numerically identical. The finite grids illustrate the role of the accumulation point; they do not assume a Yang–Mills spectrum.
