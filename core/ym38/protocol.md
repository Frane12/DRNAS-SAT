# YM38 · Longest wave in pure SU(2)

Finite-volume Monte Carlo pilot. Periodic isotropic four-dimensional Wilson action, β=2.35, spatial sizes 4, 8 and 16, temporal extent 16. Doubling only the spatial size halves the lowest nonzero momentum. Distances and energies are in lattice units. This uses the Euclidean path integral and does not identify β with the Hamiltonian ratio in YM37.

Two independent Markov chains per size, one cold and one Haar-hot. Seeds are 380000 + 100L plus 0 for cold or 1 for hot. Each uses 160 thermalization sweeps followed by 512 measurements, every full heatbath sweep. Conditional SU(2) scalar density is proportional to sqrt(1-z²)exp(β|staple|z), sampled by rejection with a tilted uniform proposal, followed by a uniform axis. Spatial APE smearing is simultaneous on the three directions within a time slice, 3 iterations and weight 0.5. The scalar operator sums the three smeared spatial plaquettes with Fourier phase at the plaquette center, momentum one unit along one spatial axis. Translational symmetry makes the ensemble one-point function zero at this nonzero momentum. The correlator uses complex-conjugate products and averages the three momentum directions and all time origins. Vacuum energy cancels in the transfer-matrix correlator.

The primary energy diagnostic solves the periodic one-state cosh ratios for separations (1,2) and (2,3). Compare estimates with paired delete-one-block jackknife errors. Report a **provisional plateau** only when C(1), C(2), C(3) exceed three standard errors and the two energies agree within two errors of their correlated difference, with block sizes 32 and 64. Require hot/cold consistency within three combined errors for mean plaquette and C(1..3) at block 32. A missing plateau gives UNRESOLVED. Passing this screen does not prove single-state dominance, thermalization or a continuum gap. C(0) is excluded from energy extraction. Primary comparison uses the (2,3) energy if all sizes pass. Ratios near 1/2 would motivate further refinement; errors, actual lattice dispersion, physical scale matching and operator overlap must also be considered.

Build with g++ -O3 -std=c++17 wave.cpp -o wave. Run ./wave 4 123 0 0 check for the local action and gauge-invariance tests. Run ./wave L SEED 160 512 cold (or hot), redirecting stdout to L4_cold.csv etc. Analyze the archived pilot with python3 analyze.py pilot. No smearing in time, no quarks and no imposed dispersion relation.

### Independent follow-up

After inspecting the pilot, fix a second series with independent seeds 381000 + 100L plus 0/1, 256 warmup sweeps, and 8192, 4096 and 1536 measured sweeps per chain at L=4,8,16 respectively. The shorter-wavelength correlator was the noisiest late-time measurement in the pilot. The action, operator, error analysis and primary windows stay fixed. Analyze the follow-up separately without pooling or selecting the better of two results. The archive stores block means from consecutive groups of 16 sweeps; they suffice to reconstruct every reported mean, error and consistency check. The two sets of underlying raw files can be regenerated using the listed seeds and sample counts (the standard library and compiler can affect random sequences). Prefix follow-up raw filenames with confirm_. Run python3 analyze.py pilot or python3 analyze.py confirm on the archived blocks; add --pack-raw to rebuild the blocks from the respective CSVs.
