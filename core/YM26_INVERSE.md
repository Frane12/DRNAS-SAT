# YM26 — plaquette-specific inverse and curvature comparison

Research argument; independent mathematical peer review needed. Keep
the YM25 invariant norm with weight E_j J_j on the same finite open
cubic grids or periodic grids L>=4, d=2,3,4. Write q=2(d-1), u=|k|/alpha,
x=q u, t=k/(3 alpha), F1=t sum W_p. B is the YM24/YM25 bilinear map,
with norm constant b=8/3. No truncation of representations.

## Sharper linearization bound

For a plaquette p all four spins are 1/2, J_p=2, and the Fourier
coefficient trace norm of W_p is <=16. For any nontrivial invariant
input tuple of G, J>=2, so the output weight is at most J+2<=2J.
In the charged-edge estimate for B(F1,G), distinguish the two charges.
For fixed output edge r, the charge to plaquettes containing r gives
at most

  3 * 2 * 16 |t| * q * (4/2) * (2/3) ||G|| = 128 q |t| ||G||.

Here each shared edge derivative in the G sum is controlled by
k_e<=2 E_k/3. For the charge to G tuples containing r, first sum
plaquettes over each shared edge; at most q with spin 1/2. Then
sum_e k_e <=2 E_k/3, giving

  3 * 2 * 16 |t| * (q/2) * (2/3) ||G|| = 32 q |t| ||G||.

As in YM24, projection to mean-zero output and inverse Casimir are
absorbed by the norm; possible double counting only enlarges the bound.
Therefore ||2 B(F1,.)|| <= (320/3)x = l. When l<1, the Neumann
series yields M=(I-2B(F1,.))^-1 with ||M||<=m=1/(1-l).
This is a volume-independent norm estimate in the stated small-coupling
range, NOT an inverse estimate for arbitrary F or weak coupling.

## Recentered preconditioned correction

The exact equation for X=F-F1 is X=M[B(F1,F1)+B(X,X)].
The YM25 defect bound is eta=c u^2, with
c=(324q+2624q(q-1))/9. For q in {2,4,6}, c<=250q^2.
Choose x<=1/160 and r=9x. Then l<=2/3, m<=3, and
m(eta+b r^2)<=1398x^2<=9x; 2mbr<=144x<=9/10.
Thus the preconditioned map is a contraction in the closed radius-r
ball. The Picard error after n iterations from zero is bounded by
m eta (2mbr)^n/(1-2mbr). No iteration coefficients are generated by
the current implementation. At zero coupling take X=0 directly.

## A sharper curvature bound

Do not bound the known F1 by its conservative Fourier norm.
YM21's direct Wilson Hessian estimate gives
|Hess F1(v,v)|<=2q|t| |v|^2 = (2/3)x |v|^2.
YM25's Fourier estimate bounds Hess X by (2/3)r.
Since Ric=1/2 in the radius-2 metric, Bakry-Emery and the
exact ground-state transform give the original KS gap

  gap >= alpha [1/2 - (4/3)(x+r)] >= 5 alpha/12.

The result is uniform in finite lattice size at fixed spacing, but
does not construct a continuum theory. It improves the sufficient
magnetic/electric ratio from 1/(240q) to 1/(160q) and the conservative
boundary gap from 4 alpha/15 to 5 alpha/12. Both improvements rely on
the analytic premises in YM21-25. The C++ task and independent Python
auditor check only rational sufficient conditions and source binding;
no proof assistant or coefficient generator verifies the derivation.
